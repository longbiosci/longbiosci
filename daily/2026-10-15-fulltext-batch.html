③ [（全文补选）] ## Introduction Frontotemporal lobar degeneration (FTLD) is a neurodegenerative dementia that demonstrates heterogeneous age of onset, clinical symptomology, and neuropathology.1 Around 10-15% of all FTLD cases are caused by autosomal dominant variants, with 13-50% caused by hexanucleotide repeat expansions in C9orf72.2,3 Both fundamental research on C9orf724 and preclinical models of C9orf72-associated FTD/amyotrophic lateral sclerosis in neurodevelopmental contexts5–7 have shown that C9orf72 is important for typical neurodevelopmental processes, including regulating cortical and thalamic volume development, neural stem cell proliferation, self-renewal, and maintenance, neuronal survival, synaptogenesis, and axonogenesis.
④ [（全文补选）] The question of whether C9orf72 repeat expansions may affect neurodevelopment in FTLD is thus being increasingly explored,8–14 with efforts underway to test this hypothesis in human youth carriers of genetic FTLD variants.15,16 In asymptomatic young adult carriers of pathogenic C9orf72 repeat expansions aged 18-30 years, arguably the last window of neurodevelopment where synaptic pruning, myelination, and maturation processes remain prominent,17 clinical neuroimaging studies have consistently observed smaller thalamic volumes at the group level.8,10,11 The earliest average age at which smaller thalamic volumes have been detected in young adult expansion carriers compared to non-carriers was 26 years old.10 Longitudinal modelling of cortical and subcortical volumes in young adult C9orf72 expansion carriers across two international FTLD cohorts extended these findings, and did not find an interaction with with age across 18 to 30 years old, supporting the hypothesis that thalamic volume differences may have neurodevelopmental origins.8 Studies of slightly older asymptomatic C9orf72 expansion carriers (aged 39.8±11.1 years) found differences in average gray matter volumes, white matter integrity, and neuropsychological test scores,18 as well as smaller volumes at the level of specific thalamic nuclei, with earliest differences affecting the laterodorsal, lateral geniculate, and pulvinar nuclei in carriers aged 43.9±11.7 years.19 A natural hypothesis arising from both preclinical and clinical neuroimaging literature, then, is that if volume differences within specific thalamic nuclei are present in young adult C9orf72 expansion carriers, the potential underlying cellular and molecular mechanisms could reflect neurodevelopmental and/or FTLD-related neurodegenerative processes.
⑤ [（全文补选）] As the thalamus is reliably the earliest region where volumetric differences are observed in C9orf72-associated FTLD,8,10,11 understanding its early developmental characteristics may provide a framework for investigating mechanisms contributing to C9orf72-associated FTLD vulnerability and resilience.
