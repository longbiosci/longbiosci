二甲双胍保护 LPS 肺损伤：多组学指向肠源代谢特征

研究问题

However, whether metformin attenuates LPS-induced pulmonary inflammation through coordinated remodeling of the gut microbiota and host-microbe metabolites remains insufficiently understood.

方法

Six-week-old SPF male C57BL/6J mice were obtained from GemPharmatech (Nanjing, China).；Mice were housed three per cage under specific pathogen-free conditions in a temperature-controlled room (21 ± 2 °C) with a 12 h light/dark cycle and free access to food and sterile drinking water.；Following a one-week acclimatization period, mice were randomly assigned to four groups: control (CON), LPS, metformin 200 mg/kg (MET 200 mg/kg), and metformin 400 mg/kg (MET 400 mg/kg).；Mice in the MET groups received metformin by oral gavage once daily for 7 days.

核心结论

These results identified a set of gut-serum synchronized metabolites associated with metformin-mediated improvement of lung inflammation, without establishing their colonic origin or causal role.；Histopathology and Immunostaining Lung and colon tissues were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E).；In the assessment of colonic-content samples, approximately 50 mg of sample was homogenized with 500 μL of 80% ice-cold methanol and incubated at –20 °C for 30 min. ‖ 结论：In conclusion, this study suggests that metformin alleviates LPS-induced pulmonary inflammation and colonic injury in association with coordinated remodeling of gut microbiota, intestinal luminal metabolites and circulating metabolites.

局限

The biological identity of several metformin-responsive metabolites provides plausible mechanistic clues for future studies.；The correlation networks were based on nominal p values and should be considered hypothesis-generating.；Future studies using metformin-only controls, post-LPS therapeutic dosing schedules, antibiotic depletion, fecal microbiota transplantation, germ-free animals, AMPK-pathway perturbation, isotope tracing, targeted metabolomics, and metabolite supplementation will be needed to test the causal structure and clinical translatability of this gut microbiota–metabolite–lung axis.
