胃肠药物使用与老年新发抑郁症状

来源：medRxiv ｜ 版本 v1 ｜ 上线日期 2026-10-28 · 预印本（pharmacology and therapeutics）：Gastrointestinal medication use patterns and incident depressive symptoms among adults aged 50 years or older: a prospective multicohort study · DOI 10.64898/2026.10.26.26366451 · 通讯 Shupeng Wang ＠ Shenzhen University（Shenzhen University） · 来源：《预印本全文级解读_2026-10-31》（观察哨45）

时间标签：数据日期 2026-10-28 · 生成时间 2026-10-31 05:30（Asia/Shanghai）｜ 档位：观察哨45 ｜ 窗口 T-3 ｜ 上游评级 C

这项研究想回答什么

老年胃肠药物使用是否与新发抑郁症状相关？

方法与数据

CHARLS、HRS 与 SHARE 三队列，Cox 比例风险模型（cluster-稳健标准误、多重插补）+ 随机效应 meta 合并，并用阴性/阳性对照药物与 Fine-Gray 竞争风险回归评估特异性与生存偏倚（两波分析 32,686 人、8,069 起事件）。

核心结论

以下所有数字均可在官方 abstract 原文（见第 7 节）中逐字核对；上游未给出的定量一律写「未给出」，不估算、不换算。

  • 一句话贡献：三队列基线分析纳入 50,467 名 ≥50 岁社区人群（中位年龄 59–65 岁，女性占 44.4%–56.8%），中位随访 6–11 年，发生 16,896 起新发抑郁事件；基线使用胃肠药物合并 HR 1.31（95% CI 1.21–1.41，I²=29%），持续使用 1.34（95% CI 1.22–1.47，I²=0%）。

局限与未核验项

观察性设计，适应证混杂与残余混杂难以排除；两波分析样本量缩减至 32,686 人，起始（HR 1.27）与停用（1.25）效应接近，提示可能与基础疾病而非药物本身相关。

补全文时优先核：胃肠药物的具体分类（PPI/抗酸/促动力）、阴性对照药物的选择理由、两波分析样本缩减的影响。

对本领域的价值

老年抑郁与躯体共病用药直接关系健康寿命（healthspan），多队列设计有一定说服力，值得跟进。

编辑研判

多队列设计的说服力值得认：CHARLS/HRS/SHARE 三队列 50,467 名 ≥50 岁者、16,896 起事件、中位随访 6–11 年，基线胃肠药物合并 HR 1.31（1.21–1.41，I²=29%）、持续使用 1.34（1.22–1.47，I²=0%），并用阴性/阳性对照药物与 Fine-Gray 竞争风险回归处理特异性与生存偏倚。但它自己给出了最有力的反解释：起始（HR 1.27）与停用（1.25）效应接近，提示关联更可能与基础疾病/适应证而非药物本身相关。老年抑郁与共病用药直接关系健康寿命，值得跟进，但不要写成药物不良反应。

官方摘要原文（逐字，供回溯）

展开 api.biorxiv.org / api.medrxiv.org 返回的 abstract 原文

Background: Depressive symptoms contribute substantially to disability and loss of independence in later life, and gastrointestinal illness frequently co-occurs with affective vulnerability. However, evidence linking gastrointestinal medication use to subsequent depressive symptoms remains largely confined to single countries and cross-sectional assessments. We investigated whether baseline gastrointestinal medication use and longitudinal two-wave medication-use patterns are associated with incident depressive symptoms across three prospective ageing cohorts. Methods: We analysed community-dwelling adults aged 50 years or older from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), the US Health and Retirement Study (HRS), and the Survey of Health, Ageing and Retirement in Europe (SHARE) who scored below cohort-specific depressive-symptom thresholds at baseline, followed for a median of 6-11 years. Baseline medication use was compared with non-use; two-wave patterns comprised continued non-use (reference), initiation, discontinuation, and persistent use at both waves. Cohort-specific Cox proportional hazards models with cluster-robust standard errors adjusted for sociodemographic, behavioural, and chronic health covariates (multiple imputation for missing data); estimates were synthesised using random-effects meta-analysis. Negative- and positive-control medications and Fine-Gray competing-risk regression models evaluated specificity and survival bias. Results: The baseline analysis included 50,467 participants (median age 59-65 years; 44.4%-56.8% female) with 16,896 incident depressive events; the two-wave analysis included 32,686 participants with 8,069 events. Pooled adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) were 1.31 (95% CI 1.21-1.41; I2 = 29%) for baseline use and 1.34 (95% CI 1.22-1.47; I2 = 0%) for persistent use; initiation (1.27, 1.13-1.43) and discontinuation (1.25, 1.11-1.40) showed consistent risk elevations across cohorts. Antihypertensive and lipid-lowering medications (negative controls) showed null associations with incident depression, whereas psychiatric medications (positive control) showed the expected strong relationship (pooled HR 1.99, 95% CI 1.79-2.22). The association remained robust after further adjusting for continuous baseline subthreshold depressive symptoms (pooled HR 1.21, 95% CI 1.14-1.27; I2 = 0%), and medicated digestive disease carried higher risk than untreated digestive disease (pooled HR 1.18, 95% CI 1.05-1.32; I2 = 0%). Accounting for competing mortality did not alter the estimates. Conclusions: Baseline and persistent gastrointestinal medication use were consistently associated with increased risk of incident depressive symptoms across three cross-national ageing cohorts with negligible heterogeneity. These findings support integrating routine affective screening into medication reviews for older adults receiving gastrointestinal pharmacotherapy, while maintaining guideline-directed management of digestive conditions.
