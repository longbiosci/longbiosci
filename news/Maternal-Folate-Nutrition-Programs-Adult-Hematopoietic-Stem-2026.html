官方摘要原文（逐字，供回溯）

展开 api.biorxiv.org / api.medrxiv.org 返回的 abstract 原文

Folic acid fortification for the prevention of neural tube defects has been a cornerstone of public health policy for nearly three decades, but its broader consequences for other developing organ systems, particularly the hematopoietic system, are poorly studied. Hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) emergence overlaps developmentally with neural tube closure, and folate-dependent one-carbon metabolism is critical for nucleotide synthesis, cellular methylation, and redox balance, positioning folate as a candidate regulator of HSC programming. Using dietary models of maternal folate deficiency or supplementation in mice, we demonstrate that maternal folate acts as a dose-dependent developmental tuner of hematopoiesis, programming adult offspring HSC function through divergent mitochondrial and chromatin states. Maternal folate deficiency impaired adult HSC self-renewal, associated with mitochondrial defects, disrupted cristae, elevated proteotoxic stress and a stress-adapted chromatin landscape. In contrast, maternal folate supplementation enhanced HSC self-renewal accompanied by improved mitochondrial quality control and CDC42-associated organelle remodeling, and established chromatin states permissive for stem cell maintenance. Fetal HSC metabolomics identified shifts in one-carbon intermediates, pyruvate and lipid pools, suggesting that divergent fetal metabolic states provide substrate-level inputs that drive persistent and opposing chromatin programs in offspring HSCs. Our findings establish maternal folate availability as an unappreciated regulator of lifelong hematopoietic output demonstrating that a single maternal micronutrient, folate, is sufficient to program adult HSC function and regenerative capacity through persistent metabolic and mitochondrial programming.
