研究问题

We previously demonstrated that a soy protein-based diet rich in phytoestrogens (i.e., “SOY”) reduces adiposity, adipose tissue inflammation, and IR in low aerobically fit female rats with and without ovarian function, yet whether the mechanism(s) for those benefits require E2 signaling remains unknown.

方法

Randomly cycling female (C57BL/6J background) αKO and βKO mice were pair-housed in a thermoneutral environment (26–28 °C) on a 12 h light/ 12 h dark cycle.；ERα wildtype (WT) controls were used to validate replication of previous studies [16,32] and underwent all treatments similar to the αKO and βKO mice.；All mice were initially provided with a low phytoestrogen content (i.e., “washout”) diet (150–250 mg/kg; #2018, Teklad Diets; Madison, WI, USA) and water ad libitum.；One week post-OVX mice were randomized to receive one of two custom-made diets: a phytoestrogen-free (CON; <15 mg/kg phytoestrogens) or a phytoestrogen-rich diet (SOY, 585 mg/kg phytoestrogens [genistein and daidzein; aglycone equivalents]) purchased from Harlan Laboratories Inc.

核心结论

SOY reduced final BW in the βKO mice by ~17%, which was not evident in the WT (0%) or αKO (6%) mice.；This associated with a trend toward lowered fat mass (↓39%; p = 0.07) in the βKO mice, whereas smaller reductions in WT (↓10%) and αKO (↓5%) did not approach statistical significance. ‖ 结论：Future studies should address mechanisms, including brain-mediated mechanisms that may help explain SOY’s protective effects on systemic metabolism and behavior, as well as perform these studies on aging male mice.

局限

Future studies should use a high-fat diet combined with SOY to determine whether SOY has anti-inflammatory effects in that setting.；However, the study was limited by the following: (1) an imperfect ERβ knockout model, which displays lack of ERβ DNA binding, but not non-genomic signaling pathways; (2) insufficient data to make definitive conclusions on WAT inflammatory populations; (3) lack of head-to-head comparison to young mice, and/or age-matched male mice, to truly assess and differentiate effects of sex and aging; and (4) lack of dose response curves and in vitro functional assays.
